# NHL_Stats

This is a page for my NHL Draft project. I was unsatisfied with how players were rated, specifically goalies. Anecdotally, I felt that ESPN's system was overvaluing goalies, and my strategy was to not draft a goalie and pick one up after the draft. I felt hey simply do not score enough points with the ruleset we use to justify picking them in early rounds. My thesis centers around the spread of point earners within positions. With our ruleset in the 2025-2026 season, Nathan Mackinnon (the top earner) was about 3.8 standard deviations above the performance of an average Center in the NHL in terms of fantasy points. In contrast, the best Goalie (Logan Thompson) was about 1.9 standard deviations above the average Goalie.

This is a rather large discrepancy. Via my analysis when ranking players based on z_score for fantasy points earned within their position, Logan Thompson was ranked 38th. I believe when you are picking players, you are picking both for the points they earn, and how much better they are than others within their position. E.g., it often makes sense to draft a Defenseman who is predicted to earn 350 points rather than a Center who is predicted to earn 380 because of how they compare to their position average. Based on the lower earning potential, and more importantly the tighter spread around the mean, drafting a goalie early does not make sense. The tighter spread around the mean for goalie performance suggests you can wait to pick a goalie later in the draft (or never, and pick one undrafted) and you can expect far more similar performance to the first goalie picked than you could expect with this strategy for any other position.
  
The 'combined_20252026' csv file contains this rough analysis, with a 'total_score' value representing the product of a player's z-score of their fantasy points earned (compared to others in the same position) and their fantasy points earned. This 'total_score' should balance the impact of fantasy points earned and z-score in player ranking. This is based on retrospective data from the 2025-2026 season with data from the NHL API. My next step is to create a prediction system like what is offered on many other fantasy apps, and ultimately to make this available on a web-based application (e.g., Streamlit) as a draft aid.
